**The National Center for Environmental Compliance (NCEC)** oversees how facilities in Saudi Arabia comply with the Environmental Law, issued by Royal Decree No. M/165 dated 19/11/1441H, and its executive regulations. To know what your facility must do, you need to know which regulations NCEC applies, what each one requires, and how to show you comply.

This guide brings together the executive regulations that matter most to facilities, summarises what each requires, and links it to the service that helps you comply, with a link to the official text of each regulation.

## What does NCEC do?

NCEC was created as part of the reorganisation of the environment sector in the Kingdom. Its objectives include stronger environmental oversight, fewer pollution sources, control of water, air and soil quality, environmental impact review, environmental licensing and inspection ([Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/national-center-for-environmental-compliance)). In practice, a facility meets NCEC at four points:

- **Before starting:** activity classification, an environmental impact assessment where needed, and the permit to construct.
- **Before and during operation:** the permit to operate and its conditions, including monitoring and reporting.
- **While operating:** inspection and environmental audit, and review of submitted data.
- **When there is a violation:** recording, penalties and an obligation to correct.

## Where to start: the environmental permits regulation

The permits regulation is the door to every other requirement. It classifies activities by environmental impact into **three categories**: the first for activities with minor impacts that stay within the site, the second for moderate impacts, and the third for the highest ([Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News2212020.aspx)). The category then sets the impact assessment and the permits to construct and to operate that a facility needs.

**What to do:** confirm your category, read the conditions of your permit line by line, and turn them into a list of obligations, each with an owner and a date. More in our [guide to environmental permits](/insights/environmental-permits-saudi-arabia-guide/).

## The media regulations: air, noise, water and soil

These regulations set the **standards** your facility's measurements are compared with, so compliance rests on correct, documented monitoring.

### Air quality

The [Executive Regulation for Air Quality](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=17812) sets ambient air standards, including for PM10 and PM2.5, and emission standards for stationary sources. Its list of violations includes failing to carry out annual tests on stationary sources not under continuous monitoring, failing to notify NCEC of emergencies, and installing or operating ambient air monitoring networks without a licence or through an unlicensed service provider.

**What to do:** list your stacks and stationary sources, confirm which need continuous monitoring and which need periodic testing, and check that whoever runs your monitoring is licensed. For sites that need continuous dust and noise data, Selorin rents a [dust, noise and weather monitoring station](/equipment/dust-monitoring-station/) with installation and weekly and monthly reports, and supplies [continuous emissions monitoring systems (CEMS)](/equipment/cems/) and [ambient air quality monitoring stations](/equipment/air-quality-monitoring-station/).

### Noise

The [Executive Regulation for Noise](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=17835) sets noise standards and permits exceedances at construction work sites from 7:00 am to 6:00 pm ([English text](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/en/Ministry/AboutMinistry/RulesAndConditions/RulesLibrary/Docs/Executive%20Regulation%20for%20Noise.pdf)). Noisy work outside these hours therefore needs planning and measurement.

**What to do:** schedule noisy works, and measure noise at the site boundary and near sensitive receptors. See [noise monitoring](/services/noise-monitoring/).

### Water

The [Executive Regulation for the Protection of Aquatic Media from Pollution](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=25435) covers surface water, groundwater and the marine environment, with a table of violations and penalties that considers the quantity and duration of pollution and the damage caused, and an obligation to remedy it.

**What to do:** know your discharge points and their limits in your permit, and monitor them at the required frequency. See [water quality monitoring](/services/water-quality-monitoring/) and [water and sediment sampling equipment](/equipment/water-sediment-sampling-equipment/).

### Soil and contaminated sites

The [Executive Regulation for the Prevention and Remediation of Soil Pollution](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=25431) covers preventing soil pollution and treating contaminated soil, complemented by the [regulation on rehabilitation of degraded sites and remediation of contaminated sites](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=17839).

**What to do:** before buying or leasing land, and when replacing fuel tanks or closing a site, test the soil and soil gas. See [environmental due diligence](/services/environmental-due-diligence/) and [soil and soil gas sampling equipment](/equipment/soil-sampling-equipment/).

## Inspection, audit and violations

The [Executive Regulation for Environmental Inspection and Audit](https://www.mewa.gov.sa/ar/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News2122020.aspx), applied from March 2021, sets the scope of NCEC's inspection and audit work. The [regulation on recording violations and imposing penalties](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=17879) sets the enforcement procedures.

**What to do:** keep the facility inspection-ready on any day, with current records, known conditions, and earlier findings closed with documented actions. See our [guide to preparing for an environmental inspection](/insights/preparing-for-an-environmental-inspection/) and [environmental audits](/services/environmental-audits/).

## Environmental service providers: why their licence matters to you

The [Executive Regulation for Environmental Service Providers](https://istitlaa.ncc.gov.sa/ar/civil/mewa/executiveregulationsforenvironmentalserviceprovide/Pages/default.aspx) requires every provider to hold a licence before offering an environmental service; the services covered include monitoring environmental media and pollution sources, and managing, installing, maintaining and operating environmental monitoring networks. Using an unlicensed provider can put the facility itself in breach.

**What to do:** ask every provider that runs a study or monitoring for you for its licence for that specific activity, and make sure submitted reports come from a licensed provider.

## Ozone-depleting substances

The [Executive Regulation for Ozone-Depleting Substances and Hydrofluorocarbons](https://www.uqn.gov.sa/details?p=17837) covers activities, appliances and equipment involving these substances, which NCEC regulates and monitors, including the permits and licences needed before such activities.

**What to do:** inventory your refrigeration and air-conditioning equipment and its gases, and document maintenance and gas recovery. See [environmental compliance](/services/environmental-compliance/).

## In short: a five-step compliance plan

1. **Confirm your category** under the environmental permits regulation.
2. **Collect your obligations** from the permit, the impact assessment and the environmental management plan into one list.
3. **Build a monitoring programme** for air, noise, water and soil as they apply, with calibrated equipment and a licensed provider.
4. **Keep records and submit reports** on time. See [self-monitoring reports](/insights/self-monitoring-reports-saudi-arabia/).
5. **Assess yourself before the inspector does**, and close findings with documented actions.

> This guide is a summary for orientation. The binding text of each regulation is the one published in Umm Al-Qura or by the official authorities, and regulations may be amended after publication.
