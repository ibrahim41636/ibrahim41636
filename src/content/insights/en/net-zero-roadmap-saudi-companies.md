With the COP31 climate conference taking place this month, net zero is back on board agendas across Saudi Arabia: do we have a plan, and where do we start? A **net zero roadmap** is not a slogan or a target year in the annual report. It is a documented plan of work that starts from actual emissions data and ends with targets that can be measured and tracked.

This guide sets out the practical steps to build one, and the mistakes that undermine its credibility.

## Why does your company need a plan now?

Saudi Arabia has announced a net zero target for 2060 under the Saudi Green Initiative. Beyond the national target, pressure on companies comes from several directions:

- **Investors and banks** ask about climate risk and transition plans, especially for sustainability-linked finance.
- **Large customers** ask suppliers for emissions data and reduction plans, because a supplier's emissions are part of their Scope 3.
- **Export markets:** mechanisms such as the [EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)](/insights/eu-cbam-saudi-exporters/) make emissions data part of trade.
- **Disclosure:** sustainability and ESG disclosure guidance is expanding, and disclosure needs a plan behind it.

## Step 1: Inventory emissions and set a base year

You cannot reduce what you don't measure. Start with a greenhouse gas inventory under the GHG Protocol:

- **Scope 1:** direct emissions from fuel, processes and owned vehicles.
- **Scope 2:** indirect emissions from purchased electricity.
- **Scope 3:** value-chain emissions such as purchased goods, transport and use of sold products.

Then choose a **base year** that represents normal operations, against which targets will be measured. Our guide to a [first GHG inventory](/insights/ghg-inventory-scope-1-2-3/) covers this step in detail, and our [GHG and carbon accounting](/services/ghg-carbon-accounting/) service delivers it.

## Step 2: Set targets

A good roadmap sets **interim targets**, not a single distant one:

| Target type | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Near-term target | Cut a set share of emissions within a few years | Turns the commitment into actions and budgets now |
| Long-term target | Reach net zero by a set year | Sets the overall direction of the transition |
| Scope 3 targets | Work with suppliers to cut value-chain emissions | Often the largest part of the footprint |

Recognised international frameworks for science-based targets can guide this, adapted to your sector and market.

## Step 3: Reduction pathways

This is where the roadmap turns from numbers into projects. Common pathways in Saudi Arabia:

- **Energy efficiency:** cooling, lighting, motors, compressed air and insulation, often the fastest payback.
- **Renewable electricity:** on-site solar, as our guide to [solar for factories](/insights/commercial-solar-factories-saudi-arabia/) explains, or renewable power purchasing where available.
- **Fuel and process changes:** switching to lower-emission fuels or electrifying some processes.
- **Fleet and logistics:** route optimisation and a gradual move to electric vehicles where it fits.
- **Less waste:** cutting wasted materials also cuts the emissions embedded in them.

Each pathway has a cost, a return and a timeline, so projects are ranked on a **marginal abatement cost curve** that compares cost with emissions avoided, starting with the most viable. [Feasibility studies](/services/renewable-energy-feasibility/) help assess renewable energy projects along this path.

## Step 4: Residual emissions and offsets

After every reduction measure, some emissions remain that are hard to eliminate. Only here do **carbon credits** come in, with three rules:

1. Reduce first; offset only what remains.
2. Prefer high-quality, verifiable **carbon removal** credits.
3. Disclose reductions and offsets clearly and separately.

## Step 5: Governance, disclosure and tracking

A plan nobody owns does not get delivered. It needs:

- **An executive owner** and a working team with authority and budget.
- **Performance indicators** tracked every year: absolute emissions, emissions intensity per unit of output and renewable energy share.
- **Precise disclosure** in the sustainability report, avoiding overstated language and backed by verifiable data, which our [ESG advisory and sustainability reporting](/services/esg-advisory/) supports.
- **An annual review** whenever the business, technologies or requirements change.

## What mistakes undermine a net zero roadmap?

- Announcing a target year without an inventory or interim targets.
- Ignoring Scope 3, even though it may be the largest part of the footprint.
- Relying on carbon credits instead of real reduction.
- A plan with no budget or owner.
- Claims in marketing or reports that go beyond what the data supports.
