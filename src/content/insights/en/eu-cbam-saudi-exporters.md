The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) puts a carbon price on certain goods imported into the European Union, based on the greenhouse gas emissions released to produce them, known as embedded emissions. For Saudi exporters of aluminium, iron and steel, fertilisers, cement and hydrogen, the direct answer is this: the EU importer files the CBAM declaration and pays through CBAM certificates, but what it pays depends on emissions data that only your installation can produce. Now that CBAM has entered its definitive period, a Saudi plant that wants its real performance counted needs to monitor emissions at installation level, calculate the embedded emissions of each product per tonne and have that data checked by a verifier accredited for CBAM.

This guide does not quote CBAM deadlines, thresholds or certificate prices, which the European Commission sets and updates; check the official sources at the end before you plan around a date.

## Who reports under CBAM, and what is the Saudi producer's role?

CBAM places the reporting duty on the EU side, but the information starts at the plant.

| Party | Role under CBAM | What it needs from others |
|---|---|---|
| EU importer (authorised CBAM declarant) | Declares the embedded emissions of imported CBAM goods and surrenders the corresponding CBAM certificates | Embedded emissions per product from the producer, verified where actual data is used |
| Saudi installation operator | Monitors emissions, calculates the embedded emissions of each product and makes the data available to buyers | Metering, production records, supplier data on precursors, an accredited verifier |
| Accredited CBAM verifier | Checks the operator's methodology, data and calculations and issues a verification report | Access to records, staff and, where required, the site |

The practical point: you file nothing with the EU, but your data is what the declaration is built on. Verification of installation data is arranged by the operator, not the buyer, so a buyer who cannot get verified figures from you has to fall back on other options.

## Which Saudi exports does CBAM cover?

CBAM covers goods in a defined set of sectors that includes aluminium, iron and steel, fertilisers, cement and hydrogen. Coverage follows customs classification, the EU Combined Nomenclature (CN) code of each product, not the way a company describes its business. In practice:

- **Check each product line, not the company.** One plant may ship both covered and uncovered products.
- **Look beyond primary products.** Some semi-finished and downstream steel and aluminium products are covered through their CN codes.
- **Account for precursors.** If you make a covered product from purchased CBAM goods, such as steel billet, aluminium ingot, clinker or ammonia, their embedded emissions count towards your product, and you will need data from those suppliers.
- **Recheck every year.** The scope can be amended by EU legislation, so compare your product list with the Commission's current sector guidance.

## What installation-level emissions data will EU importers request?

Expect a structured data request from each EU buyer, often in its own template, asking broadly the same thing: what was produced, how, and with what emissions.

| Data item | What it includes | Usual source at the plant |
|---|---|---|
| Installation details | Name, location, operator contact and the production processes on site | Site and corporate records |
| Products and CN codes | Each good exported to the EU with its customs classification | Sales, export and customs documents |
| Production route | The process steps from raw material to finished product | Process flow diagrams and operating manuals |
| Production quantities | Tonnes of each good produced in the reporting period | Production logs and weighbridges |
| Direct emissions | Emissions from fuel combustion and industrial processes attributed to each good | Fuel meters, material analyses, continuous monitoring |
| Electricity use | Electricity consumed per production process and the emission factor applied | Sub-meters, utility invoices, supplier data |
| Precursors | Quantities of CBAM-covered inputs and their embedded emissions | Supplier communications and verified supplier data |
| Specific embedded emissions | Tonnes of CO2 equivalent (CO2e) per tonne of good | Your calculation model |
| Method and evidence | Monitoring approach, factors, assumptions and the verification report | Monitoring documentation and verifier output |

Two working rules help: answer every buyer from one controlled dataset rather than separate spreadsheets that drift apart, and record electricity use per process for all covered products, because indirect emissions from electricity count for some CBAM goods under the Commission's sector guidance.

## How should a Saudi installation monitor emissions for CBAM?

The aim is a defensible figure in tonnes of CO2e per tonne of product, and most installations combine the approaches below.

### Calculation-based monitoring

Emissions from most sources are calculated from activity data and calculation factors:

- **Standard method:** the quantity of each fuel or material entering a process, such as natural gas, coke, limestone or anodes, multiplied by its emission factor, net calorific value and oxidation or conversion factor.
- **Mass balance:** carbon entering the process in fuels and materials minus carbon leaving in products, by-products and wastes. It suits complex processes, such as integrated steelmaking, where carbon moves through many streams.

Accuracy depends on calibrated flow meters and weighbridges, fuel and material samples analysed at a documented frequency, and retained laboratory certificates.

### Measurement-based monitoring

Where emissions leave through a defined point such as a stack, continuous emissions monitoring systems (CEMS) measure gas concentration and flue gas flow directly. This matters most for some non-CO2 gases, such as nitrous oxide (N2O) from nitric acid production at fertiliser plants.

### Where the emissions come from in each sector

| Sector | Main sources to monitor | Data to have ready |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminium | Anode consumption, furnace and casting fuels, perfluorocarbons (PFCs) from smelting | Anode and fuel records, electricity per process |
| Iron and steel | Reducing agents such as natural gas or coke, electrode and carbon additions, lime, reheating furnaces | Fuel and material balances, precursor data for DRI, pig iron or purchased billet |
| Fertilisers | Natural gas used as feedstock and fuel for ammonia, N2O from nitric acid | Gas metering and analyses, stack monitoring, product splits |
| Cement | Limestone calcination in the kiln, kiln fuels including alternative fuels | Clinker production, raw meal and fuel analyses, clinker bought from others |
| Hydrogen | Feedstock and fuel in steam reforming of natural gas, or electricity for electrolysis | Feedstock metering, hydrogen output, electricity source records |

Check how the CBAM calculation rules treat each gas and stream for your product before you build the model.

### Attributing emissions to products

Define the production processes in the installation first, then assign every emission source, precursor and electricity flow to a process. Shared utilities, such as an on-site boiler or power plant, need a documented allocation key. Divide the result by production to reach the specific embedded emissions per tonne.

### Data quality controls

Verifiers look for a controlled data trail, not only numbers. Prepare a written monitoring methodology, calibration records for every meter used, a procedure for data gaps such as meter failures, and version control for the calculation model, signed off by a named owner.

## Actual data or default values: what is at stake?

Where verified installation data is not available, the EU importer uses default values published by the European Commission. Default values are not based on your plant. If your actual emissions per tonne are lower than the default for your product, verified installation data is the only way to show it, and that figure becomes part of your commercial offer when buyers compare suppliers.

Default values can still be a stopgap while monitoring matures, or for small, irregular shipments, so agree with each buyer which approach it will take.

## How does CBAM verification work for a plant outside the EU?

Actual emissions data supplied for CBAM must be checked by a verifier accredited for CBAM. A general sustainability assurance provider does not qualify, so confirm the accreditation scope before signing. A verifier will typically:

1. Review your monitoring methodology and production processes, and plan sampling based on the risk of misstatement.
2. Trace figures from source records, such as fuel invoices, meter readings and laboratory results, to the final per-tonne value.
3. Check the allocation between processes, precursor data from suppliers and the electricity emission factor used.
4. Visit the site where required, to see measurement points and data handling.
5. Report findings, including misstatements or non-conformities you must correct.

Verification looks back over a full reporting period, so records must exist from its start; rebuilding a year of data afterwards is slow and often fails. An internal review beforehand catches most gaps early.

## What should a CBAM readiness plan for Saudi exporters include?

| Step | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Map exposure | List products sold into the EU, their CN codes, buyers and volumes | Register of CBAM products and buyers |
| 2. Set boundaries | Define the installation, its production processes and the goods each makes | Boundary and process map |
| 3. Choose methods | Select a calculation or measurement method for each source and document it | Monitoring methodology |
| 4. Close data gaps | Install or calibrate meters, set sampling plans, request precursor data | Gap log with owners and dates |
| 5. Build the model | Calculate direct, applicable indirect and precursor emissions per tonne | Controlled calculation model |
| 6. Test internally | Run an internal review that mirrors the verifier's checks | Readiness report and corrections |
| 7. Engage a verifier | Appoint a verifier accredited for CBAM; agree scope, timing and site access | Verification plan and contract |
| 8. Align with buyers | Agree data template, delivery dates and update cycle with each EU buyer | Documented data-sharing arrangement |

Appoint one owner for the whole file: CBAM data crosses production, maintenance, procurement, finance and sales, and can stall between them.

## How does CBAM data relate to your GHG inventory, LCA and net-zero plan?

CBAM data complements your wider climate work rather than replacing it:

- **Corporate GHG inventory:** a Scope 1 and 2 inventory under the GHG Protocol or ISO 14064-1 covers the whole organisation; see our guide to [building your first GHG inventory](/insights/ghg-inventory-scope-1-2-3/). CBAM uses much of the same source data, organised by installation, process and product. Build both from one data system so the totals reconcile.
- **Life cycle assessment (LCA):** a product carbon footprint under ISO 14040/14044 uses system boundaries that differ from CBAM rules, so an existing study is a useful input, not a ready CBAM number. [Life cycle assessment](/services/life-cycle-assessment/) remains valuable for product claims and for finding emission hotspots across the value chain.
- **Reduction:** every tonne cut at the installation lowers the embedded emissions your buyer pays for. Fuel efficiency, process changes and the electricity source become commercial levers; our [solar feasibility guide for factories](/insights/commercial-solar-factories-saudi-arabia/) covers one of them. A [net-zero roadmap](/services/net-zero-advisory/) can rank these options in line with Saudi Arabia's 2060 net-zero target.
- **Disclosure:** keep CBAM figures consistent with reporting under the Saudi Exchange ESG Disclosure Guidelines or IFRS S2. [ESG advisory](/services/esg-advisory/) helps you tell one consistent story across reports.
