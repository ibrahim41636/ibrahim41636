Electric vehicles are becoming more common in Saudi Arabia, and residents of compounds, office tenants and hotel guests are starting to ask where they can charge. Before buying the first charger, building managers need to understand the **EV charger installation requirements** for private sites. A successful project starts with the site's electrical capacity, safety requirements and approvals, and only then the choice of charger.

This guide covers what a compound or building manager needs to know before installation, and the questions to ask contractors.

## What should you check first?

**Available electrical capacity.** Every charger adds load to the existing connection, and several chargers running together in the evening can exceed what is left on the transformer or main panel. Every plan therefore starts with:

- **A load study:** the site's actual consumption, especially summer peaks when air-conditioning load is highest.
- **Spare capacity:** the gap between the connection rating and the highest recorded load is what can go to charging without an upgrade.
- **Future demand:** how many electric cars do you expect in two or five years? Designing only for today means rework later.

If capacity is short, the options are more power from the electricity company, smart load management, or on-site solar and batteries. The answer is often a mix of these.

## Which chargers suit compounds and buildings?

| | AC charger | DC fast charger |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Homes, offices, hotels, long parking | Fleets, public sites, short stops |
| Charging time | Hours | Usually minutes to under an hour |
| Power needed | Lower | Much higher |
| Equipment and cabling cost | Lower | Higher |

For most residential compounds and office buildings, **AC chargers** are the practical choice because cars stay parked for hours. If you run delivery or service vehicles that must return to work quickly, you may need one or more DC chargers. This trade-off is central to the design of [EV charging stations](/services/ev-charging-stations/).

## What are the regulatory and technical requirements?

Requirements depend on the type and size of the site, but they usually cover four areas:

1. **Electrical work:** cabling, protection and earthing must follow the Saudi Building Code and be installed by a qualified electrical contractor.
2. **Equipment conformity:** chargers, cables and connectors must conform to the standards approved in the Kingdom, with conformity certificates.
3. **Location and parking:** municipal and Civil Defense requirements may apply to charging areas, such as dedicated and marked bays, ventilation in enclosed and basement car parks, safety distances and user access.
4. **Grid connection:** extra capacity or a separate meter goes through the electricity company's procedures.

**Important:** these requirements are evolving as the EV market grows, and new guidance may be issued. Always confirm the current versions with the relevant authorities before final design, rather than relying on what applied to an earlier project.

## How can you avoid an expensive transformer upgrade?

**Smart load management** is the key tool. Instead of every charger running at full power all the time, the system shares the available capacity between active chargers and lowers charging automatically when the building's load rises. You get more chargers on the same connection.

You can also:

- **Schedule charging** overnight, when air-conditioning load drops.
- **Add solar** on carports or roofs to cover part of daytime charging, with [solar energy systems](/services/solar-energy-systems/).
- **Use battery storage** to shave peaks with [battery energy storage](/services/battery-energy-storage/), where fast charging is needed on a limited connection.

## How should managers plan for growth?

The most expensive mistake is installing two chargers today without thinking about the next ten. Good planning includes:

- **Spare conduits:** ducts or cable routes ready for more bays, because digging again costs far more.
- **Panel space:** breakers and boards that allow more chargers without replacing the panel.
- **Usage and billing policy:** who pays for the electricity, and is it charged per resident or tenant? Smart chargers log consumption per user and make settlement simple.
- **Maintenance:** a clear contract, fault response times and charger software updates.

## What should you ask a contractor?

Before accepting a quote, ask for written answers to:

- Was a load study done on the site's real data?
- What is each charger's rating, and does the quote include load management?
- Does the equipment conform to the standards approved in the Kingdom, and which certificates come with it?
- Who handles the electricity company procedures and the required approvals?
- What warranty covers the chargers and installation, and what is the maintenance plan?
- How can the system grow, and what does each extra charger cost?
